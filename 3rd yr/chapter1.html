The Internet and World Wide Web

“The Internet is becoming the town square for the global village of tomorrow.” — Bill Gates

The Internet developed through years of research, technological advancements, and the need for computers to communicate and share information. Its development eventually led to the World Wide Web, which made it possible to access information through webpages and interactive links.

Guide Question

When and why was the first computer network developed?

Page 1

1. ARPANET

Just as World War II accelerated the development of modern computers, the Cold War fueled the development of the Internet.

Although scientists who worked on the network wanted to share computer resources, the U.S. Department of Defense funded it as a research project to enhance national defense.

The Cold War climate in the United States was marked by fear of Russian communist expansion. This fear increased after Russia tested its first atomic bomb in 1949 and launched Sputnik, the first space satellite, in 1957.

ARPA

The Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA) was created in February 1958. Its creation was directly connected to the launching of Sputnik and the recognition of the need to advance military technology through research and development.

2. NSFNET

ARPANET evolved into NSFNET, which later evolved into the Internet. Along the way, the Internet passed from government control to private enterprise and commercialization.

The National Science Foundation (NSF) was founded by Congress and President Truman in 1950 to promote the progress of science, advance national health, prosperity and welfare, and support national defense.

By the late 1970s, ARPANET was having a tremendous impact. The National Science Foundation began developing a high-speed network to replace ARPANET.

This main network, also known as the backbone, connected smaller regional, national, and international networks. Between 1990 and late 1994, parts of NSFNET were sold to major telecommunications companies until the Internet backbone became completely commercial.

3. The Internet

While ARPANET was just one network, it quickly evolved into a network of networks.

According to Vint Cerf, the term Internet came from the inter-netting project and the system of networks that emerged from the research.

Important Concept

The Internet is a global network of interconnected computer networks. No single person or organization owns the entire Internet.

4. Internet2

Since the commercialization of the Internet, there has been an effort to develop a new non-profit network that returns to the original purpose of the Internet: education and research.

Internet2 was developed by the research and education community in 1996. It provides leading-edge network capabilities and unique partnership opportunities.

By bringing research and academia together with technology leaders from industry, government, and the international community, Internet2 promotes collaboration and innovation.

5. Computer Network Timeline

The following timeline presents important events that contributed to the development of computer networks and the Internet.

1945–1950s

Cold War and Nuclear Development

  • The United States was the only nuclear power until 1949, when Russia tested its first atomic bomb.
  • In 1952, the United States developed the hydrogen bomb, followed by Russia in 1953.
  • The 1950s was the era of bomb shelters and school bomb drills.
1957

Sputnik Launch

  • Russia launched Sputnik, the first man-made satellite, into space.
  • The event increased concerns about Russian dominance in science and technology.
  • Government funding for scientific research and development increased during the Cold War.
1964

Packet-Switching Technology

Packet-switching technology was funded by the U.S. Department of Defense. It aimed to develop a communication network without a central headquarters that could be destroyed during a nuclear attack.

1969

ARPANET Created

ARPANET was created by the Pentagon's Advanced Research Projects Agency. It connected computer sites at four research universities so scientists could share files.

1972

Network Development

  • ARPANET had 37 sites or nodes, including universities and government research facilities.
  • Network protocols were developed to allow computers to transfer data.
  • TCP/IP converts messages into packets and reassembles them at the receiving end.
  • The first public demonstration of email allowed users to communicate.

6. Early Net Applications

Studies have shown that communication is one of the major uses of the Internet. Until the 1990s, communication and other computer network applications were mostly text-based.

Electronic Mail (E-mail)

Email was first developed in 1971 by Ray Tomlinson of ARPANET. It uses the format user log-in name @ host computer name.

Telnet

Telnet allows a direct connection from one computer to a distant server.

FTP

File Transfer Protocol (FTP) is used to transfer files, particularly large files, between computers.

7. Vinton Cerf: Father of the Internet

Vinton Cerf played a major role in developing the protocols and telecommunications standards used in Internet packet-switching technology.

In 1976, Cerf was a DARPA program manager in charge of packet technology and inter-networking research.

TCP/IP

Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol (TCP/IP), developed by Cerf's team at ARPA, checks data packets for errors and reassembles them in the correct sequential order at the destination site.

These packet-switching standard protocols allow consistent and universal data transmission.

8. J.C.R. Licklider: Visionary from Missouri

Joseph Carl Robnett Licklider was a professor of psychology of communications who worked at DARPA's Information Processing Techniques Office.

In 1960, Licklider envisioned a future thinking center that would combine the functions of libraries with advances in information storage and retrieval.

He imagined a network of connected centers that people could access through communication lines.

In his 1965 book, Libraries of the Future, Licklider discussed how computers could provide an automated library with simultaneous remote access for many people through a common database.

9. Computer Connectivity: Client-Server

Computers are connected together in networks organized as clients and servers.

Clients

Clients are personal computers or computer workstations that request information and services.

Servers

Servers are large central computers that hold collections of data and programs. They help connect computers to other networks.

Internet Service Providers (ISPs)

ISPs provide Internet connectivity through larger servers. Examples mentioned in the source include AOL and Comcast Cable.

10. What is the World Wide Web?

Tim Berners-Lee developed the World Wide Web in late 1990 while working at CERN, the European Particle Physics Laboratory in Geneva, Switzerland.

The Web added the ability to display graphics, multimedia, and hypertext or interactive links to the Internet.

Ideas That Helped Build the Web

Vannevar Bush

Wrote "As We May Think" in 1945, describing the concept of a machine called Memex for cross-referencing information.

Doug Engelbart

A Stanford researcher who invented the computer mouse, first demonstrated in 1968.

Ted Nelson

Wrote Literary Machines in 1981, describing Xanadu, a non-linear hypertext format with embedded links.

HTML: Hypertext Markup Language

Berners-Lee and his colleagues at CERN developed HTML, the computer language used for the Web. They also developed a browser program to access and display Web content.

11. Early Web Browsers

Early browsers helped users access the Web and view hypertext, graphics, and other webpage content.

1992

Lynx

A text-only hypertext browser developed by Michael Grobe and other students at the University of Kansas.

1993

Mosaic

An early successful browser capable of displaying hypertext and graphics. It was developed at the National Center for Supercomputing Applications.

1994

Netscape

An early commercially available browser produced by Mosaic Communications Corporation.

1995

Internet Explorer

Released by Microsoft in 1995.

2004

Mozilla Firefox

Released by Mozilla as a free, open-source browser in 2004.

CHAPTER 01 VOCABULARY

Glossary of Terms

This glossary contains important terms and definitions discussed in Chapter 1: Internet and World Wide Web Fundamentals.

1. ARPANET

Advanced Research Projects Agency Network. An early computer network funded by the U.S. Department of Defense.

2. ARPA

Advanced Research Projects Agency. An agency created in 1958 to support research and technological development.

3. NSF

National Science Foundation. An organization established to promote scientific research and development.

4. NSFNET

A high-speed network developed by the National Science Foundation to connect research and educational institutions.

5. Internet

A global network of interconnected computer networks that allows devices to communicate and share information.

6. Internet2

A research and education networking community that develops advanced network capabilities.

7. Packet Switching

A communication method that divides data into smaller packets before sending them across a network.

8. TCP/IP

Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol. A collection of communication protocols used to transmit data across networks.

9. E-mail

Electronic Mail. A method of sending and receiving digital messages through computer networks.

10. Telnet

A protocol that allows a user to connect to and communicate with a remote computer.

11. FTP

File Transfer Protocol. A protocol used to transfer files between computers over a network.

12. Client

A computer or workstation that requests information or services from a server.

13. Server

A computer that stores data, programs, and resources and provides services to connected clients.

14. ISP

Internet Service Provider. A company or organization that provides access to the Internet.

15. World Wide Web (WWW)

A system of interconnected webpages and digital resources that users can access through the Internet.

16. HTML

Hypertext Markup Language. The standard markup language used to structure content on webpages.

17. Hypertext

Text that contains links allowing users to navigate from one document or webpage to another.

18. Web Browser

A software application used to access, retrieve, and display webpages and other Web content.

19. Lynx

An early text-based Web browser that displays webpage information without graphical images.

20. Mosaic

An early graphical Web browser that helped popularize the use of graphics and hypertext on the Web.

21. Netscape

An early commercially available Web browser released in the 1990s.
